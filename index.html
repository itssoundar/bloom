What it does

Everything between spotting it and filing it.

Bloom folds the screenshot → crop → annotate → export → paste loop into one panel in your browser toolbar.

Capture from the toolbar

Whole page, a scrolling region, or a single element — triggered from where you already are, without leaving the tab.

Mark it up in place

Arrows, boxes, freehand marker, and typed notes — drawn on the live page, before anything gets flattened.

Arrives as vector layers

Each frame and stroke lands in Figma as an editable path. Drag an anchor, change a stroke weight, resize without softening an edge.

Keep a local copy

Pull down a PNG or SVG whenever you need the capture somewhere other than Figma. No separate export step.

Paste it anywhere

Drop a marked-up capture into Slack, Notion, a Jira ticket, or an email — same panel, same shortcut.

Nothing to maintain

No terminal, no local server, no dependency to keep current. Conversion and the Figma connection both run server-side.

Why it matters

Most capture tools hand you a picture. Bloom hands you geometry.

When a stroke leaves your browser, Bloom doesn't flatten it. Every mark is resolved into anchor points and Bézier curves before it reaches the canvas.

✕

A pasted screenshot is one flat image. The annotations are baked in — you can't move an arrow, and it softens the moment anyone zooms.

✓

A Bloom capture is a real Figma frame. The markup sits on its own layer, selectable and editable independently.

✓

Grab a single anchor and drag it. Recolor one stroke. Delete one arrow. Nothing else in the frame shifts.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Everything worth knowing before you request a UAT slot.

How is this different from just pasting a screenshot into Figma?

A pasted screenshot is a single flattened image. Bloom sends a real frame with the annotation layer kept separate, so each arrow, box, and marker stroke stays selectable and editable on the canvas. You can move one anchor without disturbing anything else.

Do I need to install or run anything besides the extension?

No. There's no CLI, no local server, and no dependency to keep up to date. Vector conversion and the Figma connection both run on our servers — the extension is a thin capture layer.

Can I use captures outside Figma?

Yes. Any capture can be downloaded as a PNG or SVG, or copied straight into Slack, Notion, a Jira ticket, or an email from the same panel — no separate export step.

What can I capture?

Three modes: the current viewport, a scrolling region you drag out, or a single element you point at. All three are triggered from the toolbar without leaving the tab.
